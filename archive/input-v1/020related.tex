\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Knowledge distillation.} Classical distillation fits a student to a
teacher's output distribution on a fixed corpus \citep{hinton2015distilling}
or to sequences the teacher generates \citep{kim2016sequence}. On-policy
formulations supervise text generated by the model being trained, following
the imitation-learning argument for learning on the learner's own state
distribution \citep{ross2011dagger,agarwal2024gkd,gu2024minillm}; the idea has
been applied to reasoning models, including with a model as its own teacher
\citep{zhao2026opsd}, and knowledge fusion extends distillation to several
source models \citep{wan2024fusellm}. Recent work asks which rollouts
on-policy distillation should use, selecting a few hard problems
\citep{hou2026whatmatters}, weighting problems by the student's pass rate
\citep{xu2026paced}, or balancing student-generated states across teachers
\citep{hou2026uniopd}. All of these are optimization procedures. \method keeps
two of their ingredients, a match-the-teacher objective and statistics
gathered on model-generated text, and removes the optimization by solving the
objective layer by layer. Our finding that the composition of the rollouts
decides the outcome is a closed-form counterpart of theirs.

\paragraph{Model merging.} Weight averaging across fine-tuning runs improves
accuracy at no inference cost \citep{wortsman2022soups}; task arithmetic
composes updates relative to a common initialization \citep{ilharco2023task},
and TIES-Merging and DARE reduce interference among summed updates
\citep{yadav2023ties,yu2024dare}. Spectral methods exploit the matrix structure
of updates \citep{gargiulo2024tsv,marczak2025isotropic,sun2026resmerge}. These
methods read weights only. Weight-space edits also target reasoning models,
to amplify one reinforcement-learning run
\citep{yang2026doesscalingreinforcementlearning} or to make a model more
efficient \citep{ma2026s3spectralnullspaceswap}; we combine runs on different
domains. Fisher merging and RegMean bring in data through parameter
importance and through input second moments respectively
\citep{matena2021fisher,jin2023regmean}. \method uses RegMean's closed form;
what differs is what it is fed and what it is used for. RegMean was introduced
for models fine-tuned on supervised tasks, with statistics computed on each
task's training inputs. The specialists here are reinforcement-learned
reasoners whose inputs are overwhelmingly their own long generations, so the
statistics have to come from rollouts, and we show that on AIME25 unfiltered
rollouts gain nothing over task arithmetic, whereas filtered ones recover the
specialist.

\paragraph{Other training and evaluation settings.} Multi-domain fine-tuning
models interactions among samples during training \citep{liang2025boosting};
distributional Pareto-optimal reinforcement learning studies trade-offs across
objectives \citep{NEURIPS2023_32285dd1}. Policy optimization has also been
studied with interactive guidance
\citep{liu2026socraticpopolicyoptimizationinteractive}, while anomaly-guided
learning addresses imperfect demonstrations \citep{AAMAS'22:AGPO}. Related
choices about supervision include adaptive sample scheduling for direct
preference optimization \citep{huang2025adaptive} and reward models that go
beyond semantics \citep{Huang2026RealTimeAR}. Arena-based LLM evaluation
\citep{liu2025elo} and medical vision-language work on adaptive causal
reasoning \citep{lin2026models} and latent-memory evolution
\citep{zhu2026medsynapsevbridgingvisualperception} concern other evaluation or
application settings.
